# Task 3.3 — Training and fine-tuning process for FMs

> Domain 3 (28%). Smaller than 3.1 and 3.4, but it owns a distinction the exam loves: **which training method for which need**, and what makes fine-tuning data good.

---

## 1. The four training stages

| Stage | Data | Labeled? | Who does it | Cost | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-training** | Massive, broad, general corpus | No — **self-supervised** | Model providers | **$M+**, months | Build general language/world capability from scratch |
| **Continued (continuous) pre-training** | Large **domain-specific** corpus | No — still unlabeled | Advanced customers | High, weeks | Teach the model a domain's vocabulary and patterns |
| **Fine-tuning** | Small–medium **labeled** task examples | **Yes** — prompt/response pairs | Customers | Medium, days | Teach a specific task, style, format, or behaviour |
| **Distillation** | Outputs from a large "teacher" model | Generated by the teacher | Customers | Medium up front, then cheap | Compress a big model's capability into a small, fast, cheap one |

### 🔍 Pre-training vs. CONTINUED pre-training

They share a name and a method, and differ in **who does it, on what, and starting from where**.

| | **Pre-training** | **Continued (continuous) pre-training** |
|---|---|---|
| **Starts from** | **Nothing** — random weights | An **already pre-trained** FM |
| **Data** | Massive, broad, **general** corpus (much of the internet) | Large, **domain-specific** corpus (your industry's documents) |
| **Labeled?** | ❌ No — self-supervised | ❌ **No — still unlabeled.** This is the key shared trait |
| **Who does it** | **Model providers** (Anthropic, Amazon, Meta) | **Customers** with deep domain corpora |
| **Cost / time** | **$millions, months** | High, weeks — but a tiny fraction of pre-training |
| **Produces** | A general-purpose FM | A **domain-specialized** version of that FM |
| **Teaches** | Language, reasoning, world knowledge from scratch | **Industry vocabulary, terminology, and domain patterns** |

**The mental model:** pre-training is *raising and educating a person from birth*. Continued pre-training is *sending an already-educated adult to immerse in a new field* — they keep everything they knew and add a specialist vocabulary.

> **The exam scenario that names it**: a medical device company wants AI-generated product overviews to use **industry-specific terminology**, and has **"a large volume of unlabeled industry-specific standards and research."**
> → **Continued pre-training.** Two signals together: **large volume** + **unlabeled**.
>
> **Why the others fail there:**
> - **Instruction-based fine-tuning** — needs **labeled** data; the corpus described is unlabeled ❌
> - **RAG** — injects terminology as *context per query*, but **doesn't improve the model's own ability** to use that terminology in future responses ❌
> - **Prompt engineering** — a sample in one prompt **doesn't persist** beyond that prompt or series ❌

> **The RAG-vs-continued-pre-training subtlety worth holding onto:** RAG *retrieves* terminology at query time; continued pre-training *absorbs* it into the weights. If the question asks the model to **learn** the language of a domain (not look it up), that's continued pre-training.

### The distinction that gets tested

| | Continued pre-training | Fine-tuning |
|---|---|---|
| Data | **Unlabeled** raw domain text | **Labeled** prompt/response pairs |
| Volume | Large corpus | Smaller curated set |
| Teaches | Domain *knowledge and vocabulary* | Task *behaviour and format* |
| Example | Feed it 10 years of medical journals | Give it 500 examples of correctly formatted discharge summaries |

### Model distillation
A large, accurate **teacher** model generates responses; a small **student** model is trained to imitate them. The result approaches the teacher's quality *on the target task* at a fraction of the inference cost and latency.

**Amazon Bedrock Model Distillation** automates it: you pick a teacher, pick a student, and supply **only prompts** — the service generates the synthetic responses from the teacher and fine-tunes the student for you. Amazon Nova Premier is positioned as a teacher model.

AWS's published figures for distilled models: **up to 500% faster and up to 75% cheaper** than the original, with **less than 2% accuracy loss** for use cases like RAG.

> **Exam cue:** "maintain quality but dramatically cut inference cost at high volume" → **distillation**.
>
> **Note the tradeoff:** the student matches the teacher on the **specific use case it was distilled for**, not in general. You're trading breadth for cheap speed on one task.

## 2. Methods of fine-tuning

### 🚦 FILTER 1 — is it even a TRAINING method?

**Do this before anything else.** If the question says **"train the FM on our data"**, two of the usual options are eliminated instantly, because **they never touch the model's weights at all**:

| Technique | Changes the model? | Is it a training method? |
|---|---|---|
| **Prompt engineering** | ❌ No — only the input | ❌ **NO.** AWS is explicit that prompt engineering is **not a method to train** an FM on domain-specific data |
| **RAG** | ❌ No — only adds retrieved context | ❌ **NO.** AWS is explicit that RAG is **not a training method** |
| **Instruction-based fine-tuning** | ✅ Yes — weights | ✅ Yes |
| **Domain adaptation fine-tuning** | ✅ Yes — weights | ✅ Yes |
| **Continued pre-training** | ✅ Yes — weights | ✅ Yes |
| **RLHF** | ✅ Yes — weights | ✅ Yes |
| **Model distillation** | ✅ Yes — the student's weights | ✅ Yes |
| **Pre-training** | ✅ Yes — from scratch | ✅ Yes |

**Also weight-neutral (inference-time only):** inference parameters (temperature, Top K, Top P), Guardrails, agents/tool use, context engineering, prompt caching. → **[What model weights are, and the full list](../01-ai-ml-fundamentals/01-ai-concepts-terminology.md)**

> 🚨 **The classic error here.** When a requirement says *"needs to **train** a foundation model on business data,"* **RAG** is the most common wrong pick. RAG is excellent, and it *is* the right answer to "the model needs current/private facts" — but it **is not training**. The word **"train"** should have eliminated both RAG and prompt engineering before you compared anything else.
>
> **Watch for the collision with the other rule you've learned:** "RAG for facts, fine-tuning for behaviour" is still true — but it only applies **once you know a training method is wanted**. **"Train" outranks it.** If the stem says train/fine-tune/adapt the *model*, RAG is off the table regardless of how facts-shaped the scenario looks.

### 🎯 FILTER 2 — which training technique?

**Memorize this table.** The exam repeatedly offers these against each other, and they are distinguished by **what data you have** and **what you're trying to change**.

| Technique | Data required | Data **size** signal | What it teaches | Question cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Domain adaptation fine-tuning** | Domain/industry-specific data | **SMALL / limited** (e.g. **1 GB**) | **Industry terminology and domain knowledge** — makes generated content use the right vocabulary | "industry-specific terminology", "**limited** domain-specific data", "**unique vocabulary**", "specialize for our field" |
| **Instruction-based fine-tuning** (a.k.a. **instruction tuning**) | **Labeled** examples (prompt→response pairs) | Small–medium, **labeled** | **How to FOLLOW DIRECTIONS** — and performance on a **specific downstream task**, in the format you want | "**understand and follow directions**", "**guided responses**", "specific task", "labeled examples", "adapt for a downstream task" |
| **Continued (continuous) pre-training** | **UNLABELED** domain text | **LARGE volume** | Deep domain vocabulary and patterns, absorbed from raw corpus | "**large volume of unlabeled** industry standards/research/documents" |
| **RLHF** | **Human preference rankings** of outputs | — | **Alignment** with human goals, values, and preferences | "human feedback", "align with human goals/needs", "helpful and harmless" |
| **Pre-training** (from scratch) | Massive general corpus | **Enormous** | Everything, from nothing | Essentially never the answer — **$millions, months** |

> **⏱️ Time and size are decisive signals.** *"1 GB dataset, training and evaluation in under 3 weeks"* → **fine-tuning**. Full pre-training takes **months** and a vastly larger corpus, so a deadline in weeks rules it out on its own. AWS describes domain adaptation fine-tuning as adapting a pre-trained FM with **limited** domain-specific data — **faster and more cost-effective** than fully training one.

> **Domain adaptation vs. continued pre-training — refined by data size**:
> - **1 GB / "limited" / "unique vocabulary"** → **domain adaptation fine-tuning**
> - **"large volume of unlabeled" standards, research, documents** → **continued pre-training**

> 🔑 **The cleanest way to split these four:instruction tuning changes how the model BEHAVES. Domain adaptation and continued pre-training change what the model KNOWS. RLHF changes what the model VALUES.**
>
> So *"the AI teaching assistant must understand and follow directions and provide guided responses"* → **instruction tuning**. Nothing there is about knowledge or vocabulary; it's entirely about direction-following behaviour. The words **"directions"** and **"instructions"** are close to a literal word-match for the technique.

> **How AWS frames each one:**
> - **Domain adaptation fine-tuning** customizes a pre-trained FM on domain-specific information, improving how it handles industry terminology.
> - **Instruction-based fine-tuning** uses labeled examples to improve performance on a specific downstream task. **Not** a way to learn terminology.
> - **RLHF** incorporates human feedback so the model aligns with human goals and preferences. **Not** a way to learn terminology.

### ⚠️ The hard one: domain adaptation vs. continued pre-training

Both teach **industry terminology**, and the same terminology goal can have **two different correct answers**. The differentiator is **how the question describes the data**:

| Scenario wording | Answer |
|---|---|
| *"has access to a **large volume of unlabeled** industry-specific standards and research"* | **Continued pre-training** — the explicit mention of a large *unlabeled corpus* is the tell |
| *"wants to ensure generated content includes industry-specific terminology"* (no dataset described) | **Domain adaptation fine-tuning** |

> **The rule:** if the question hands you a **big unlabeled corpus**, that's **continued pre-training**. If it just states a domain/terminology goal with no dataset emphasis, it's **domain adaptation fine-tuning**. And if it mentions **labeled examples for a task**, it's **instruction-based fine-tuning** — which the exam explicitly says is *not* the terminology answer.

### The other methods

| Method | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Transfer learning** | The general principle underneath all of this — take a model trained on one task and adapt it to a related one, reusing learned representations instead of starting over |
| **PEFT / LoRA** | Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning — update only a small set of added parameters instead of all weights. Far cheaper and faster; the standard practical approach |
| **Supervised learning** | A general training paradigm (learning from labeled data), **not an FM customization technique**. It appears as a distractor in FM questions — the FM-specific version is *instruction-based fine-tuning* |

> **Catastrophic forgetting** is the risk to know: fine-tuning too aggressively on narrow data can degrade the model's general capability. Mitigate with mixed training data and conservative hyperparameters.

## 2b. 🔧 Fine-tuning on Amazon Bedrock — what actually happens

### The five facts the exam tests

| # | Fact | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fine-tuning creates a NEW CUSTOM MODEL** | The base model is untouched. You end up with a **separate custom model** that is yours — this is the *outcome* of the job |
| 2 | **It needs only a SMALL dataset** compared to pre-training | Hundreds to thousands of examples vs. the original pre-training corpus. That's what makes it a practical customization option |
| 3 | **NOT every FM on Bedrock supports fine-tuning** ⚠️ | Model customization is **not supported by all FMs**. Check before you plan around it |
| 4 | **Fine-tuning requires LABELED data** | ⚠️ Contrast: **continued pre-training requires UNLABELED data**. This pair is tested constantly |
| 5 | **No vector store is involved** | A vector store is **RAG** machinery. Fine-tuning does **not** need one to "retain data through training" |

> 🚨 **A common misconception:** *"a vector store is necessary to retain data throughout training."* **Vector stores belong to RAG, not to fine-tuning.** RAG stores embeddings so it can retrieve passages at query time; fine-tuning just runs a training job over labeled examples and writes new weights. **If a fine-tuning question mentions a vector store, it's wrong.**

### 📏 WHY a small dataset is enough — and when you need a large one

**The rule:** the data you need is proportional to **how much NEW the model has to learn.**

| Technique | Data volume | Why that much |
|---|---|---|
| **Prompt engineering** | **None** (or 2–10 examples inside the prompt) | Nothing is learned or stored — you're only phrasing the request |
| **RAG** | Your document corpus, **any size** | Not *training* data at all — material to retrieve from at query time |
| **Fine-tuning** | **SMALL** — often hundreds to a few thousand labeled examples, or ~1 GB of domain data | The model **already knows language, reasoning, and the world**. You're **nudging existing weights** toward a behaviour, format, or vocabulary — not building knowledge from nothing |
| **Continued pre-training** | **LARGE** unlabeled corpus (many GB) | You're teaching it a whole domain's language and patterns, which has to be *absorbed* from volume |
| **Pre-training from scratch** | **MASSIVE** — trillions of tokens, terabytes | Building **all** knowledge from random weights |

#### The key insight: "small" is relative to pre-training

This is what makes the phrase confusing. **1 GB sounds big in isolation — but next to a pre-training corpus it's negligible.** Pre-training runs on terabytes and trillions of tokens across thousands of GPUs for months. Against that, 1 GB is a rounding error, which is why it is described as a **relatively small dataset compared with the original pre-training corpus**.

So when a question says **"limited"** or **"small amount of data"**, it means *small compared to pre-training* — not small in absolute terms.

#### 🍟 Back to the burger franchise

The [analogy in Task 3.1](01-design-considerations.md) explains *why* the numbers differ:

- **A week of in-store training** (fine-tuning) works because **the new hire already knows how to cook**. You're teaching house style, not cooking. **50 examples of your wrapping standard is plenty.**
- **Culinary school for a new cuisine** (continued pre-training) needs months of immersion, because the vocabulary and technique are genuinely unfamiliar.
- **Raising a person from birth** (pre-training) takes everything.

**Fine-tuning needs little data for the same reason the week of training works: almost everything the model needs, it already has.**

#### When fine-tuning does need MORE data

Still nowhere near pre-training scale, but scale up when:

- The target behaviour is **far from** what the base model does naturally
- You're changing **broad behaviour** rather than a narrow format
- The domain vocabulary is genuinely unfamiliar — **at which point continued pre-training is the better tool anyway**

> **Quality over quantity, still.** For fine-tuning, a few hundred **clean, consistent, representative** examples beat thousands of noisy ones. That's the opposite of pre-training, where raw volume is the point.

### The steps, end to end

```
1. PREPARE DATA      Labeled prompt/response pairs, typically JSONL → upload to Amazon S3
       ↓
2. PICK A BASE MODEL  Must be one that SUPPORTS customization (not all do)
       ↓
3. CREATE THE JOB     Bedrock "custom model" / fine-tuning job; set hyperparameters
       ↓                (epochs, batch size, learning rate)
4. TRAIN              Bedrock runs the job
       ↓
   ➡️  OUTCOME: a NEW CUSTOM MODEL (base model unchanged)
       ↓
5. EVALUATE           Test the custom model against your golden dataset
       ↓
6. SET UP INFERENCE   Choose EITHER (or both):
       ↓                 • Custom model deployment → ON-DEMAND (per-token, no provisioned compute)
       ↓                 • Provisioned Throughput  → reserved capacity, billed hourly
7. INVOKE             Call your custom model like any other Bedrock model
```

> **Step 6 used to be the cost sting** — custom models once *required* reserved capacity. **On-demand deployment for customized models removed that constraint**, so fixed hosting cost is no longer automatic. Fine-tuning still costs more than RAG overall (labeling + training + re-training on every data change) — but **not** because it forces you into Provisioned Throughput.

### Fine-tuning vs. RAG — which machinery belongs to which

| Component | Fine-tuning | RAG |
|---|---|---|
| **Labeled training data** | ✅ Required | ❌ Not needed (just documents) |
| **Vector store / embeddings** | ❌ **Not involved** | ✅ **Core to it** |
| **Training job** | ✅ Yes | ❌ No training at all |
| **Changes model weights** | ✅ Yes → new custom model | ❌ No |
| **How you serve it** | **Either** a custom model deployment (**on-demand**, per-token) **or** Provisioned Throughput | Just call the base model |

## 3. Preparing data to fine-tune

The objective names the criteria explicitly — data preparation is most of the work.

| Requirement | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Curation** | Select high-quality, relevant, deduplicated examples | Garbage in, garbage out — a small clean set beats a large dirty one |
| **Labeling** | Correct, consistent prompt/response pairs | Inconsistent labels teach inconsistency |
| **Size** | Enough examples to learn the pattern — often **hundreds to a few thousand**, not millions | Too few won't shift behaviour; more isn't automatically better |
| **Representativeness** | Covers the real distribution of inputs, including edge cases | An unrepresentative set produces a model that fails in production |
| **Governance** | Provenance, licensing, consent, retention, access control | Legal and compliance exposure; also PII risk |
| **Bias review** | Balanced across demographics and categories | Biased data produces biased outputs (Domain 4) |
| **Formatting** | Correct schema — typically JSONL prompt/completion pairs in S3 | Bedrock and SageMaker require specific formats |
| **Splitting** | Hold out validation data | Detect overfitting during tuning |

> **Trap:** more data is not automatically better. **Quality, consistency, and representativeness beat volume** for fine-tuning — the opposite instinct to pre-training.

> **Governance is not paperwork here.** Fine-tuning data becomes part of the model. PII in your training set can surface in outputs, and you can't easily un-train it.

## 4. Choosing the approach

**Start with the training filter — it resolves more questions than anything else:**

```
STEP 1 ── Does the question say TRAIN / FINE-TUNE / ADAPT THE MODEL?
    ├─ YES → eliminate PROMPT ENGINEERING and RAG immediately.
    │         They never change the model. Go to step 2.
    └─ NO  → prompt engineering or RAG may well be the answer.

STEP 2 ── Which training method? Decide on DATA + GOAL:
    ├─ LABELED prompt/response pairs, or "follow directions"  → Instruction-based fine-tuning
    ├─ SMALL/limited domain data, "unique vocabulary" (~1 GB) → Domain adaptation fine-tuning
    ├─ LARGE volume of UNLABELED domain text                  → Continued pre-training
    ├─ Human preference rankings, "align with human goals"    → RLHF
    └─ From nothing, $millions, months                        → Pre-training (never the answer)
```

**The older heuristics still apply — but only *after* step 1:**

```
Does the model need CURRENT or PRIVATE FACTS?
    └─ Yes → RAG (no training at all)

Does it need to BEHAVE differently — style, format, tone, task?
    └─ Yes → Fine-tuning (labeled examples)

Does it need deep DOMAIN VOCABULARY it has never seen?
    └─ Yes → Continued pre-training (large unlabeled corpus)

Is quality fine but INFERENCE COST too high at volume?
    └─ Yes → Model distillation

Is the base model already good enough with better instructions?
    └─ Yes → Prompt engineering (always check this first)
```

**On Amazon Bedrock:** you can serve a custom model **two ways — and this is a correction to older guidance**:

| Serving option | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Custom model deployment → on-demand inference** | **Token-based pricing, pay only for what you use, no provisioned compute to set up** |
| **Provisioned Throughput** | Reserved capacity billed hourly, with optional term commitment |

**Both options exist independently and can even be used simultaneously for the same custom model.** Which is available depends on the Region, the model, your throughput needs, and cost. **Custom Model Import** lets you bring your own weights.

> ⚠️ **Common misconception (it used to be true):** *"a customized model can ONLY be used with Provisioned Throughput."* **That is now false.** On-demand deployment for customized models is supported. If an option says **"only** in Provisioned Throughput mode," it's wrong; the accurate statement is **"in Provisioned Throughput OR On-Demand mode."**

## Exam traps for this task

1. **Fine-tuning = labeled data. Continued pre-training = unlabeled domain text.** The most-tested pair in this task.
2. **Fine-tuning teaches behaviour, not facts.** Current information → RAG.
3. **Pre-training from scratch is essentially never the right exam answer** — cost and time are prohibitive.
4. **Distillation = teacher/student**, trading up-front cost for cheap fast inference.
5. **RLHF uses human preference rankings** to train a reward model that aligns the model.
6. **Data quality beats quantity** for fine-tuning; hundreds to thousands of good examples is the realistic scale.
7. **Catastrophic forgetting** is the risk of over-narrow fine-tuning.
8. **Transfer learning is the umbrella principle**, not a separate product.
9. **PEFT/LoRA updates a small parameter subset** — cheaper than full fine-tuning.
10. **Custom models can be served on-demand OR with Provisioned Throughput** on Bedrock. ⚠️ **Reject any option saying "ONLY in Provisioned Throughput mode."** Also reject **"On-Demand only with time-based term commitments"** — On-Demand has **no** commitment; term commitments belong to Provisioned Throughput.
